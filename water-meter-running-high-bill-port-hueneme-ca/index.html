Water Meter Still Running or High Water Bill in Port Hueneme, CA

A meter that keeps turning when nothing is on is the clearest sign of a leak. This guide shows how to test it and how to read what the city bills you.

Call 24/7: (805) 249-7135
By the Port Hueneme Pro Plumbing teamPublished October 29, 2026Updated October 29, 2026

Read the meter in HCF

The City of Port Hueneme meter reads in hundred cubic feet, called HCF. One HCF is 748 gallons according to the city, and one cubic foot is about 7.48 gallons. Your bill is based on how many HCF passed the meter since the last reading. A small change in HCF is a lot of water.

That is why a hidden leak can surprise you. A pinhole that loses a gallon an hour adds up to hundreds of gallons in a month, which can be a full HCF or more, depending on the leak. The meter does not distinguish between water you used and water that escaped.

Test the meter for a leak

  1. Shut off every tap and appliance, sprinklers included.
  2. Lift the lid of the meter box near the street and find the flow indicator, a little triangle or wheel on the dial.
  3. Watch it for a minute. Movement means water is going somewhere.
  4. Close the sprinkler valve if there is one. If the movement stops, the leak is in the irrigation.
  5. Close the main at the house. Movement that continues points to the pipe between that valve and the meter.
  6. If it stops, look inside the house or on the house side of the main.

Common causes of a high bill

  • A toilet flapper that leaks into the bowl all night.
  • A stuck sprinkler valve or a broken head.
  • A hidden leak in a wall, slab or buried line.
  • A leaking water heater relief valve or supply connection.
  • More people at home, a pool fill or a long period of watering.

A running toilet is the cheapest fix. Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank and wait a few minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper leaks. Our toilet repair page covers it.

Port Hueneme note: billing and who to call

The City of Port Hueneme Water Division serves the city and can be reached at 805-986-6563. Utility Billing is at 805-986-6524. The municipal code as posted says residential and commercial water charges are billed monthly, effective May 1, 2020, so a leak shows up in the bill within a billing cycle. The code defines bi-monthly as a period of no more than 66 days.

We could not find a published city leak adjustment policy. The code has a general dispute process in which the Finance Director reviews a written request within 30 days, but that is not a leak credit program. Ask the Water Division and Utility Billing about a leak adjustment, and keep your repair invoice and the meter readings from before and after the repair.

Finding the leak

If the test shows a leak inside the house or in the yard, we use pressure testing, acoustic listening and thermal imaging to find it. For leaks that show up as a wet floor, see slab leak repair, and for failures that spray, see burst pipe repair. We mark the leak and explain the repair before we open anything.

A written note from the plumber that describes the leak and the repair is useful when you ask the city about an adjustment. We provide one on request.

Prevent the next surprise

Check the meter once a month with everything off. Write down the reading on the same day each month. Replace toilet flappers every few years. Look at sprinkler heads after each landscaping visit. A habit of five minutes a month catches most leaks before they show up on the bill.

How to read the meter properly

Find the meter box near the curb, lift the lid carefully and wipe the glass. Use a flashlight, and watch for insects, because meter boxes are a common home for them.

Look for the small low-flow indicator, often a triangle or a star. It spins when even a tiny amount of water passes, which makes it the best clue for a slow leak.

Check the toilet tanks with a few drops of food coloring. If the color shows up in the bowl without flushing, the flapper leaks, and a silent toilet leak is one of the most common reasons for a high bill.
