What Temperature to Set a Tankless Water Heater in Port Hueneme, CA

The answer for most homes is 120 degrees Fahrenheit. This guide explains why, when a different setting makes sense and how Port Hueneme water changes the picture.

By the Port Hueneme Pro Plumbing teamPublished October 29, 2026Updated October 29, 2026

The short answer: 120 degrees

The Department of Energy's Energy Saver guide recommends setting a water heater to 120 degrees Fahrenheit, and the Consumer Product Safety Commission gives the same advice. A tankless heater sets its output temperature electronically, and 120 is a good starting point. It is warm enough for showers and dishes and cool enough to lower scald risk.

Many tankless units let you set a higher temperature. Higher is not better. Hotter water increases burn risk and can speed scale buildup. If you need more heat for a specific task, such as a dishwasher without a booster, check the appliance manual first.

Why scald risk matters

The Commission's data shows how quickly temperature changes injury. Exposure for five minutes at 120 degrees can cause a third-degree burn. At 130 it takes about 30 seconds, at 140 about 6 seconds and at 150 about 2 seconds. The Commission notes that older adults and children under five account for most tap-water scald injuries and deaths.

What changes with a tankless heater

A tank heater stores hot water at one temperature and mixes at the tap. A tankless heater heats on demand to the set point. If you run several taps at once, the unit may struggle to hold the set temperature at a high flow rate. That is a capacity limit, not a thermostat fault. Lowering the flow, staggering uses or choosing a larger unit are the fixes.

The Department of Energy says on-demand gas units can be about 24 to 34 percent more efficient than tank heaters in homes using 41 gallons or less per day, and about 8 to 14 percent more efficient at about 86 gallons per day. It also says they can last more than 20 years and need specialized installation by a certified technician. A lower set point adds to the savings.

Hard water and the setting

Scale forms on a tankless heat exchanger faster as temperature rises. A lower setting slows it, though it does not stop it. Descaling on a schedule is the real answer. Our tankless water heater page explains the flush and how often to do it.

Port Hueneme note: what the city says about your water

The City of Port Hueneme 2025 water quality report lists total hardness at 210 milligrams per liter in the blended water, about 12 grains per gallon by our conversion. It lists pH about 7.5 and total dissolved solids about 490 parts per million, and says the water is disinfected with chloramines, not chlorine. Scale builds in a tankless heat exchanger in water like this, which is why the maintenance schedule matters more than the setting.

We did not find a California rule that fixes the temperature setting for a home tankless heater, so this page relies on the Energy Department's Energy Saver guide and the Commission's scald table. Check your unit's manual and ask your plumber if your household has special needs.

Setting the temperature safely

  1. Check the manual for the model and the way to change the set point, usually a remote controller or the unit's panel.
  2. Set it to 120 degrees and wait for a normal use cycle.
  3. Test the temperature at a tap with a thermometer in a running stream.
  4. Raise by 5 degrees only if you cannot get comfortable hot water at normal flow.
  5. Lock the controller if children or guests might change it.

If the unit reports an error code, do not raise the temperature to solve it. Codes point to ignition, flame sensing, venting, flow or overheating problems. Scale on the exchanger and a clogged inlet screen are frequent causes.

When the setting is not the problem

The cold water sandwich, a brief burst of cold water between two hot ones, is a known tankless behavior and not a setting fault. A recirculation setup or a small buffer tank can reduce it. If hot water is slow to arrive, the pipe run to the tap is long, and a recirculation line helps there too.

Why temperature swings happen

Tankless heaters fire only when they sense flow. A very low flow, such as a trickle at a single tap, may not trigger the burner, and the water stays cool no matter what the setting says.

Mineral scale on the heat exchanger is another common cause. Scale insulates the metal, so the unit works harder and the outlet temperature can drift, which is why many manufacturers call for periodic descaling.

A tap with a mixing cartridge that has worn out can also pull cold into the hot line. Check whether the swings happen at every fixture or only one before you blame the heater.
