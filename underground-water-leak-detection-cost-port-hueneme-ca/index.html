What underground leak detection is

A buried leak is a break or a failing joint in a pressurized water line below the ground or beneath a slab. Detection is the process of locating it to within a small area, so a repair means opening a small spot instead of an entire yard. Detection and repair are separate jobs, and many companies price them separately.

Without detection, a plumber would have to guess where to dig. With it, the plumber combines a pressure test, acoustic listening and, if needed, thermal imaging or tracer gas to find the spot. The goal is a mark on the ground and a depth, not just a diagnosis.

Port Hueneme note: where your responsibility starts

We could not find a City of Port Hueneme statement on who owns the water service line between the meter and the house. The California Contractors State License Board describes plumbing work on water piping from the owner's side of the utility meter to the structure, which supports the common view that the house side is the owner's.

That is general wording and not a city rule, so ask the Water Division at 805-986-6563 if the leak is near the meter.

The city's meter guide gives a free first check: with all water off, a moving flow indicator means a leak, and closing the house main shows whether it is between the main valve and the meter. If the indicator still turns with the main closed, tell the city. The meter reads in hundred cubic feet, and one HCF is 748 gallons, so you can estimate how much is leaking by timing the reading.

Detection versus repair costs

Detection is usually the smaller part. A repair can be a short dig to patch a pipe, or the replacement of a long section. If the line is old and failing in several places, replacing it may cost less in the long run than repeated patches, which is why we explain both options. Our burst pipe repair page covers failures that show on the surface, and repiping covers whole-house replacement.

Slabs add another layer. A leak under a concrete floor needs a different repair path, from spot repair to rerouting. See slab leak repair for those options.

What insurance may and may not cover

Many policies cover sudden water damage, but not the cost of finding or fixing a slow leak that went unnoticed. Some policies cover the cost of tearing out and replacing a wall or floor to reach a leak, and some do not. Read your policy and ask your agent before the work, and keep your water bills, which show when use started to rise.

Why locating comes before repairing

Paying for a precise location usually costs less than paying for exploratory digging. A leak found within a foot or two means a smaller trench, less landscape to restore and a shorter repair.

A concrete slab, a driveway or a retaining wall changes the price of the repair far more than the price of the detection. Ask the plumber to separate the two in the quote.

If your water bill jumped, take a photo of the meter dial before and after a quiet hour. That record helps the plumber and helps you explain the jump to the utility.
