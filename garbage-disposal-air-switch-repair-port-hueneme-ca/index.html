Garbage Disposal Air Switch Repair in Port Hueneme, CA

An air switch is the small button on your countertop that starts the disposal without a wire near the sink. When it stops working, the cause is usually simple, and the safety rules are worth knowing.

How an air switch works

An air switch has two parts. A push button on the countertop or sink rim sits at one end of a thin flexible tube. At the other end, under the sink, is a small module connected to the disposal's power. Pressing the button sends a puff of air through the tube. The module senses the pressure and closes the circuit, which starts the motor. Pressing again shuts it off.

People choose air switches because they put no electrical wiring at the countertop, where water and hands are common. The trade-off is that the system has more parts that can fail than a plain wall switch, including the button, the tube and the module.

Safety first

The federal Consumer Product Safety Commission advises unplugging a wet electrical product before touching it and keeping countertop appliances away from the sink or on an outlet with ground-fault protection. It also says to turn off power at the circuit breaker and have an electrician check any appliance that shocks you. These are general appliance rules, but they apply directly to a disposal that sits under a wet sink.

  • Turn off the breaker or unplug the disposal before touching any wiring or the module.
  • Never put a hand into the grinding chamber, even when the unit looks stopped.
  • Keep the cabinet floor dry and clear of stored items while you work.
  • Test any ground-fault outlet at the sink monthly, as the Commission suggests.

A safe order of checks

  1. Switch the disposal off at the breaker.
  2. Open the cabinet and look at the air tube from the button down. A kink, a pulled-off fitting or a crack stops the signal.
  3. Press the reset button on the bottom of the disposal after the motor has cooled.
  4. Turn the power back on and press the button. If the disposal starts, the problem was a trip or a loose connection.
  5. If nothing happens, stop. The module or the wiring may need replacing, and that work belongs with a plumber or electrician.

If the disposal hums but the blades do not move, you have a jam, not a switch problem. The flywheel can usually be freed with a hex wrench from underneath after the power is off. Our garbage disposal repair page explains the steps and when to call.

Repair or replace

A button and tube are inexpensive parts and the module is a modest repair. If the disposal itself is old, rusted at the base or leaking from the housing, fixing the switch only extends a failing unit. We compare the cost of the repair to the cost of a new disposal and tell you which makes more sense. The air switch hardware can often be reused on the new unit.

When a new disposal goes in, the plumber also checks the sink drain, the trap and the dishwasher connection. A poor connection at the dishwasher hose is a common source of slow drains and odors. If the sink backs up even with the disposal working, see drain cleaning.

Before you replace the switch

Check the easy things first. Confirm the disposal has power, press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit and look for a jam by turning the grinder with a hex key from underneath.

If the motor hums but nothing happens when you press the button, the problem is more likely the disposal than the switch. If nothing happens at all, the air hose, the button or the outlet is the likelier culprit.

Always cut power at the breaker or unplug the unit before reaching into the sink opening. A disposal that starts unexpectedly is a hand injury waiting to happen.
