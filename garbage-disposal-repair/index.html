A jammed or leaking disposal can shut down the kitchen. We free the jam, fix the leak or replace the unit, and show you what to keep out of it.

A garbage disposal that hums without turning, leaks under the sink or backs the sink up needs attention before the next meal. Our line is open 24/7, and a plumber can usually reach a Port Hueneme kitchen the same day.

First, switch the disposal off at the wall switch and, if possible, unplug it or turn off its breaker. Never put your hand in the disposal, even when it looks stopped. Water and electricity share this space, so keep the area dry and use the reset steps only after power is off.

Disposal jammed or leaking: what to do first

  1. Turn off the wall switch and unplug the unit or switch off its breaker.
  2. Look for a hex socket in the center of the underside. A plumber or you with the correct wrench can turn it to free a jam.
  3. Press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit after the motor has cooled.
  4. Do not use chemical drain cleaners, which can damage the disposal and hurt whoever opens the drain next.
  5. If water is leaking from the unit, shut the cabinet supply valves and call.

The federal Consumer Product Safety Commission advises unplugging a wet electrical product before touching it and keeping countertop appliances away from the sink or on a GFCI outlet. We follow that advice on every disposal visit. If a disposal ever gives you a shock, do not touch it, switch off its breaker and call an electrician as well as us.

What should not go into a disposal

Grease is the biggest enemy. The City of Port Hueneme tells residents never to pour grease, oil or fat down a sink, because it hardens in pipes and can back up into homes. The city says to let small amounts of used cooking oil cool, then put them in the trash, and to call the Water Resource Inspector at 805-986-6661 for a disposal station for larger amounts.

  • Grease, oil and fat, which coat the line and cause backups.
  • Fibrous scraps like celery, corn husks and artichoke leaves, which wrap around the shredder.
  • Coffee grounds and eggshells in large amounts, which pack into the trap.
  • Pasta, rice and potato peels, which swell and become paste.
  • Bones, pits and hard objects, which jam the flywheel.

What affects the price of disposal repair or installation

A reset is a quick visit. A replacement involves the unit and the connection. These factors apply.

Repair or replace

A jam or reset is usually inexpensive. A cracked housing means a new disposal.

Horsepower and model

Larger, quieter units cost more.

Mounting and electrical

Some sinks need a new mount. An air switch or a new outlet adds work.

Dishwasher connection

The drain hose attaches to the disposal on many sinks and must be reconnected.

Time of day

Emergency dispatch costs more than a scheduled visit.
