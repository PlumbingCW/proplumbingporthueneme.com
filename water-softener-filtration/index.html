Water Softener and Filtration in Port Hueneme, CA

Hard water leaves scale on fixtures and heaters. We explain what the city reports, what a softener or filter can do and what it cannot.

Hard water is the story behind a lot of plumbing wear, from white crust on faucets to a water heater that rumbles. Port Hueneme's water is hard, and the city says so in its yearly report. This page explains what the numbers mean and how softening and filtration fit.

What the city reports about your water

The City of Port Hueneme Water Division supplies tap water from the Port Hueneme Water Agency plant, which blends reverse-osmosis and nanofiltration treated local groundwater with imported Calleguas water. The 2025 water quality report lists total hardness at 210 parts per million, or milligrams per liter. Dividing by 17.1 gives about 12 grains per gallon. The report lists sodium at 58 ppm and total dissolved solids at about 490 ppm. The city disinfects with chloramines, at about 2.5 ppm.

Those figures describe water at the treatment plant. Water at your tap can differ with the plumbing between. If you want your own number, test your tap and ask the Water Division at 805-986-6563 for the latest report.

Softener, filter or neither

A water softener swaps hardness minerals for sodium or potassium and removes scale at the source. A filter, such as a carbon cartridge, targets taste, odor or sediment but does not soften. Reverse osmosis at the kitchen tap treats drinking water only. These tools do different jobs, so we start with what bothers you rather than with a product.

  • If scale bothers you everywhere, a whole-house softener addresses it.
  • If chlorine or chloramine taste bothers you, a carbon filter helps and a softener does not.
  • If you only want better drinking water, an under-sink filter or reverse osmosis unit may be enough.
  • If you have a tankless heater, softening protects the heat exchanger.
  • If you are on a low-sodium diet, ask a doctor before choosing a salt-based softener.
