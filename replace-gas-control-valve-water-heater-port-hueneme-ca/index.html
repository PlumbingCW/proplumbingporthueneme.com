Replacing the Gas Control Valve on a Water Heater in Port Hueneme, CA

The gas control valve is the brain of a gas water heater, and it is also the part that handles gas. Here is how to tell it has failed and what a safe replacement involves.

By the Port Hueneme Pro Plumbing teamPublished October 29, 2026Updated October 29, 2026

What the gas control valve does

The gas control valve sits at the bottom of a gas tank water heater. It holds the dial, the pilot control and the thermostat, and it opens and closes the gas to the pilot and the burner. A thermocouple or a similar sensor tells it the pilot is lit, and if the pilot goes out the valve shuts the gas off. It is a safety device as much as a control.

When it fails, the symptoms look like other water heater problems. The difference is that testing it correctly requires care around gas, and replacing it requires gas connections that must be leak free. That is why it is a job for a licensed professional.

A thermocouple is a cheap part and is the more common culprit. Plumbers test it before blaming the valve. A new thermocouple that still fails to hold the pilot suggests the valve itself.

What a safe replacement involves

  1. Shut off the gas at the appliance shutoff and the water supply to the heater.
  2. Drain enough water to work at the valve and remove the burner assembly.
  3. Disconnect the gas line, the pilot and thermocouple lines and the old valve.
  4. Install the new valve with the correct thread sealant for gas.
  5. Reconnect everything, restore gas and test every joint for leaks.
  6. Light the pilot, check the flame and the draft and verify the safety shutoff.
  7. Refill, check the temperature and strap or confirm the strapping on the tank.

That is a summary, not a manual. The details depend on the model, and a mistake on a gas connection is dangerous. The leak test at the end is the most important step.

Port Hueneme note: licensing and gas safety

SoCalGas says to use a licensed contractor for all gas appliance and venting repairs. The California Contractors State License Board plumbing classification covers water-heating equipment, gas appliances, flues and gas connections, and maintaining gas safety and control devices. The Board also says that unlicensed work is not allowed on projects that need a building permit or cost $1,000 or more in labor and materials.

If you smell gas, SoCalGas tells customers to leave the area at once, not to light flames or operate switches and to call 1-800-427-2200 from a safe place, or 911 if anyone may be in danger. We could not find a specific California statute on homeowner gas work, so we limit our advice to what the utility and the licensing board say. Port Hueneme's Building and Safety Division at 805-986-6512 can tell you whether your job needs a permit.

Repair or replace the heater

A new control valve is a sensible repair on a heater that is otherwise sound. On a tank older than ten to twelve years, or one that shows rust at the base, the money may be better put toward a new heater. The valve is one of several parts that age together. We lay out both numbers and recommend the one that makes sense. See water heater repair and water heater replacement.

Sediment and flame quality

The City of Port Hueneme reports about 210 milligrams per liter of hardness in its 2025 water quality report, roughly 12 grains per gallon by our conversion. Hard water builds sediment on the tank bottom, where the burner heats. A sediment layer causes noise, uneven heat and sometimes soot on the burner. A heater with these symptoms and a failing control may need both a flush and a valve.

We also check venting. A heater that backdrafts can blow out the pilot and look like a bad control. The vent path, the draft and the combustion air all matter, and a closet install needs particular care.

What to do before you hire a plumber for the valve

Ask whether the replacement valve is the exact model for your heater. Valves are not universal, and a mismatched part can change the flame or the safety behavior.

Ask how the gas joints will be tested. A bubble test or an approved leak detector on every joint is the standard answer, and it should happen before the heater is left running.

Ask what happens if the valve turns out to be fine. A plumber who tests the thermocouple and the draft first will tell you honestly when the valve is not the cause.
