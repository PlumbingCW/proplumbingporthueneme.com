Emergency Leak Detection in Port Hueneme, CA

The hardest plumbing problems start with a symptom and no obvious cause. We locate the leak first, show you the evidence, and only then talk about repair.

Call 24/7: (805) 249-7135

Water that reaches the wrong place is an emergency once it soaks drywall, lifts flooring or runs your meter at night. Our leak detection line is staffed 24/7, and the first thing we ask is what you notice, because the symptom tells us where to start.

While you wait, find your main shutoff and test it. If the leak slows or stops with the main closed, the problem is on the house side. If it keeps going, the pipe may be between the shutoff and the meter, and the city should hear about that too.

Hidden leak right now: signs to look for

  • A water bill that rises with no change in how you live.
  • The sound of running water when every tap is closed.
  • A warm patch on a floor or a hot spot that appears out of nowhere.
  • Musty smell, peeling paint, bubbling plaster or stained ceilings.
  • Low water pressure at every tap, not just one.
  • Wet or sunken soil in the yard with no sprinkler running.

Check the meter yourself first

The City of Port Hueneme meter guide gives a simple test. Shut off all water use in the house and look at the meter. If the small flow indicator on the face is still turning, water is moving somewhere. The city describes how to close the sprinkler valve and then the house main in turn to narrow the source down, and it asks you to call the Water Division at 805-986-6563 if you suspect a leak.

The meter counts in hundred cubic feet, called HCF, and one HCF is 748 gallons according to the city. A slow leak of a few gallons an hour adds up across a monthly billing cycle, and the city bills water monthly. That is why a meter that moves when nothing is running deserves attention.

We combine methods because no single tool works everywhere. A pressure test says a line is leaking, an acoustic pass says roughly where, and a moisture reading confirms the spot. We mark it, explain what the repair would involve, and give you the choice of how to proceed.

Walls, ceilings and attics

Stains on a ceiling usually point to a leak above, but the water can travel along a joist before it shows. We trace upward from the stain, check supply lines, drain lines and fixtures in the room above, and test whether the water is clean supply water or drain water. The answer decides whether it is a pressurized pipe, a drain or a roof problem.

Underground and yard leaks

A buried service line can leak for months before the surface shows it. Soft ground, a green patch in a dry lawn or a damp strip along a driveway can all be clues. We locate the leak along the line and tell you the depth and the likely repair. When a long section is failing, replacing it is usually smarter than patching it, and we explain both routes with prices.

What affects the price of leak detection

Finding a leak costs a different amount from fixing it, and each depends on the situation.

Where the leak is thought to be

A fixture or a visible wall stain is quicker than a buried line or slab.

Number of methods needed

One pressure test may be enough. A stubborn leak may need several tools.

Access to pipes and meter

Clear access saves time. Crawl spaces, locked utility rooms and landscaping add effort.

Size of the property

More piping means more to test and isolate.

Time of day

After-hours dispatch costs more than a weekday visit.

Insurance often covers sudden water damage but not the cost of finding or fixing a slow leak that went unnoticed. Photograph the damage and keep your water bills, since they show when use started to rise.

You get a clear price before work starts. Call (805) 249-7135 and describe the symptom, and we can narrow the range on the phone.

What to do before we arrive for a suspected leak

  • Turn off every tap and appliance, then read the meter and read it again after 15 minutes.
  • Close the main valve at the house and check whether the meter still moves, which separates a yard leak from a house leak.
  • Do not dig or cut into a wall on a hunch, because water travels away from the leak.
  • Switch off the water heater if a leak may be on the hot side and the tank is losing water.

High water bill: when it is the meter, the pipe or the sprinklers

A high bill is not always a leak. A running toilet, a stuck irrigation valve or a pool fill can do the same thing. The meter test above separates a true leak from heavy use. If the meter is still moving with everything off, we find the pipe. If it is not, the bill needs a conversation with Utility Billing at 805-986-6524.

We have not found a published city policy that credits a bill after a repaired leak, so we suggest asking the Water Division and Utility Billing directly. Keep the repair paperwork we give you.
